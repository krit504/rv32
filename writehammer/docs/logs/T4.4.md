# T4.4 — Random Location Attack

RnLA is prior work of the author's group in the Write Hammer draft (local paper and citation in T4.2). One target is selected from eight configured locations; the LFSR selects a new location and an unpredictable dwell interval on each switch. Direct targets occupy geometry-derived D$ lines. Paper targets are arrays larger than the D$, so the sweeps force fills and dirty write-backs. Five seeds are in the registry.

Acceptance commands:

```sh
scripts/run.sh -- .venv/bin/python tests/attack_program.py --kind RnLA
scripts/run.sh -- .venv/bin/python tests/attack_mutant_control.py --kind RnLA
scripts/run.sh -- .venv/bin/python analysis/attack_runner.py --case rnla_direct_s1 --case rnla_direct_s2 --case rnla_direct_s3 --case rnla_direct_s4 --case rnla_direct_s5 --case rnla_paper_s1 --case rnla_paper_s2 --case rnla_paper_s3 --case rnla_paper_s4 --case rnla_paper_s5
```

Result: 10/10 native and RV32I caches-off/on self-checks HALT 0; 10/10 scoped broken-core runs reach self-check HALT 127; 10/10 cached wear runs terminate and exactly match golden cache replay. Direct windows span 1,931,841–1,935,038 cycles; paper windows span 2,004,312–2,037,152. PMR(all) direct spans 18.7768–23.4472 and paper 1.2029–1.2775. Numbers and plots are generated under `work/attack_run/` by `analysis/attack_runner.py` and `analysis/wear_stats.py` with provenance sidecars. Windowed movement is evaluated separately in T4.7. The PMRs are below benign `crc32`; no attack behavior was altered to change that comparison.
